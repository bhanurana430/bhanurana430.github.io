Behind the work / Independent research · Trading systems Approximately October 2024 – January–February 2025

Sentient

Could a learning agent make sense of a market? The harder question was how to test the answer.

Sentient began as an exploration of algorithmic trading: trying technical rules, price-sequence models, and reinforcement learning approaches against historical data. The research raised practical questions about data preparation, how trading decisions are represented, and whether results survive careful evaluation.

  1. 01

    Start with the baselines.

    I first investigated familiar rule-based signals and their behavior across historical data. The repository contains multiple VectorBT notebooks, indicator experiments, custom backtesting utilities, and attempts to compare strategies—not just a single final model.

    This work matters because every additional model needs something fair to compare against. A backtest can make an idea look appealing without proving that it will generalize to different market conditions.

  2. 02

    Learning changes the question.

    The project also explores sequence models and reinforcement learning. The checked-in dueling DQN separates value and advantage streams, uses a target network, and samples from a prioritized replay buffer. A custom Gym environment simulates long and short positions and combines changes in net worth with other reward terms.

    The committed training script uses a one-value observation, two actions, and 50 episodes. Other experimental scripts are not completely consistent with that setup, so those specifications should not be generalized to every iteration of Sentient.

  3. 03

    What I carried forward.

    Sentient includes working research implementations and model artifacts, but the repository does not substantiate a profitable, production-ready trading agent or a robust improvement over conventional strategies.

    That distinction shaped what interested me next: instead of assuming the model knows the right trade, build better tools to create, test, and interrogate trading rules. NeonEdge follows that product direction.

My contribution

I explored rule-based backtests, sequence-learning models, and a custom reinforcement-learning environment.
